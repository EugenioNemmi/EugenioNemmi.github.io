Department of Computer Science

Rome, Italy

I am an Assistant Professor (RTD-A) in the Department of Computer Science at Sapienza University of Rome, and part of the SystemsLab group.

I work on security and privacy, mostly through measurement. I look at how people and systems get tracked, deanonymized or scammed, and try to work out how often it happens and what can be done about it. Recently this has meant one-way arbitrage on crypto exchanges, weaknesses in Community Notes, and email tracking pixels.

I did my PhD at Sapienza with Alessandro Mei, finishing in 2022, and I have worked with people at UCSD (CAIDA), MIT, NYU and Utrecht University. I teach introductory programming and algorithms. In 2023 I co-founded DataBeers Rome, a free public event about data.

There is more in my bio, publications and CV.

what your browser just told me

Any website can read a lot of small details about your device without asking. One by one they are boring. Put together they make a browser fingerprint, which is often enough to recognise you on a later visit, even after you clear your cookies. This is yours.

Your fingerprint

A short code built from the details above. Studies of real browsers have found that combinations like this are unique for many visitors, and that is why fingerprinting gets used for tracking. Browsers such as Tor Browser and Brave push back by making their users look alike.

All of this runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere, and it is gone when you leave the page.

Eugenio Nerio Nemmi is an Assistant Professor (RTD-A) at the Department of Computer Science of Sapienza University of Rome. His research focuses on security and privacy, with work spanning blockchain fraud, privacy-preserving systems, platform integrity, user profiling and deanonymization, and Internet measurement.

## Background

He received his BSc (2015), MSc (2018, 110 cum laude), and PhD (2022) in Computer Science from Sapienza University of Rome, all under the supervision of Prof. Alessandro Mei. His PhD thesis, _"Profiling users on different platforms and detection of BGP attacks"_, combined machine learning, NLP, and authorship-attribution methods to deanonymize users across social platforms and the Dark Web with Internet-measurement techniques for detecting BGP hijacks.

After the PhD he held postdoctoral positions at Sapienza (2022–2025), working on blockchain and crypto-asset fraud, interdisciplinary applications of machine learning, and the SERICS project (Spoke 9, PNRR). He has been an Assistant Professor since September 2024. From 2016 to 2022 he was part of SystemsLab, where he designed Android applications and ticketing systems and supervised research and technology-transfer projects.

In August 2025 he was an invited visiting researcher at the Center for Secure Information Systems at New York University, hosted by Prof. Damon McCoy.

## Research interests

- **Blockchain security and financial fraud** — NFT wash trading on Ethereum, token-related frauds, and one-way arbitrage on centralized exchanges; confidential data exchange on public blockchains (MARTSIA).
- **Privacy-preserving systems** — secure, confidential, and verifiable decision support using TEEs and cryptographic protocols; email tracking pixels and web privacy.
- **Platform security and integrity** — vulnerabilities of community fact-checking (Community Notes); bot and gender detection on Twitter.
- **User profiling and deanonymization** — linking Dark Web aliases to real Internet identities, nationality and native-language profiling, machine-translation detection.
- **Internet measurement** — BGP hijacks and misconfigurations, in collaboration with UCSD (CAIDA) and MIT.
- **Machine learning for the primary sector** — generative AI for agriculture, aquaculture, and marine ecological monitoring; vegetation-index estimation.
